# Introduction

> Start here. This vault answers one research question and assumes no prior background.

## The question

**How can resource planning be optimized in an archipelagic nation that adopts a federal system of government?**

Three ideas sit inside that question:
- **Resource planning** — the five-link fiscal chain (assignment of spending, assignment of taxes, transfers, budget discipline, multi-year planning). See [[What is Resources Planning]].
- **Archipelagic nation** — a state of many islands, where fragmentation is not an inconvenience but a fiscal variable: it raises service-delivery cost and weakens the local tax base simultaneously. See [[How Federation Resources Planning Affected Archipelagos Nation]].
- **Federal system** — a constitution where sovereignty is shared between a general government and regional governments, each with guaranteed powers. See [[What is Federation]].

## Why the question is hard

The archipelagic penalty operates through **formulas**. A transfer formula weighting population and land area — a reasonable design for a contiguous country — systematically under-allocates to island regions, because islands are dispersed (high cost) and sparsely populated (low weight). Indonesia's DAU formula is the documented case: eight provinces with 72–96% water territory received less than terrestrial provinces for years. Fixing this by adding "water" to the formula does not finish the job — equalization is zero-sum in a fixed pool, and helping islands reduces mainland transfers, which provokes resistance.
