# Summary

**Research question:** How can resource planning be optimized in an archipelagic nation that adopts a federal system of government?

**Answer in one paragraph:** Federal resource planning is a five-link chain — expenditure assignment, revenue assignment, intergovernmental transfers, PFM discipline, multi-year investment planning. Archipelagic geography breaks link after link: fragmentation raises service-delivery costs while weakening the local tax base, so uniform formulas (population + land area) systematically under-allocate to island regions. The optimization is therefore not "more transfers" but **formula design**: cost-of-provision indices that include connectivity and dispersion, capacity-plus-need equalization in the Pakistan Box 11 pattern, own-revenue effort terms to prevent dependence, asymmetric archipelagic modules under a common federal accountability core, and constitutional entrenchment so the formula survives the political impulse to reclaim discretion. Indonesia's DAU/PAD experience shows every one of these failure modes in live operation; Pakistan, Sudan, Ethiopia, and Nigeria show the systemic versions.

## The five-link chain ([[What is Resources Planning]])

1. **Expenditure assignment** — each function to the tier representing its beneficiaries (Ter-Minassian 1997)
2. **Revenue assignment** — mobile/elastic/uneven bases to the center; immobile bases locally
3. **Intergovernmental transfers** — close the vertical gap; equalize the horizontal gap
4. **PFM discipline** — predictability; "when funding flows are unpredictable, service delivery units cannot plan" (Haque et al. 2026)
5. **Multi-year investment planning** — OECD 15-capacity framework

## What archipelagic geography does ([[How Geographical Problems Pushed For Federation]])

Dispersed populations, fragmented territory, high transport costs, weak agglomeration:
- **Revenue side** — limited tax base → weak own-source revenue
- **Expenditure side** — higher unit cost of service delivery
- **Formula side** — population/land-area weights under-allocate to islands (Indonesia's DAU; Kepri 96% water, 2M people across 2,408 islands)

Equalization is zero-sum in a fixed pool — "an increase of more than 80% [water weight] would not result in equitable financial capacity, as the increase of DAU of archipelagic regions would reduce those of non-archipelagic provinces" (Simandjuntak 2018).

## The seven trade-offs ([[Tradeoff With The Federation Resources Planning]))

Mainland–island · connectivity–resilience–social protection · equity–efficiency (Buchanan 1952) · autonomy–equalization (Bucovetsky 1998) · simplicity–need-sensitivity · uniformity–asymmetry (Allain-Dupré 2020) · ambition–feasibility (Seidel & Schrooten 2000). Optimization is **joint** over the formula vector, not axis-by-axis.

## The five failure modes ([[Systemic Risks & Failure Modes in Developing Federations]])

1. Decentralization without capacity → big weak government (Ethiopia)
2. Unpredictable funding → disorderly allocation (Pakistan §5.2.1)
3. Formulas that reward the strong (Sudan FFAMC; Pakistan NFC)
4. Fragmentation and intergovernmental rivalry (Nigeria; Pakistan tax base)
5. Transfer dependence crowding out local effort (Indonesia)

All five trace to one driver: "control over money enables the mobilization and maintenance of political support... predictability, strict rule-based allocation, and strong oversight can reduce discretion, contradicting political imperatives" (Haque et al. 2026, Box 9).

## The optimization ([[Translating Political Constraints to Algorithmic Rules))

```
T_i = α · Need(i) + (1-α) · CapacityGap(i) − Effort(i)
```

- `Need` includes dispersion, connectivity cost, climate exposure
- `Effort` rewards own-revenue collection (anti-dependence)
- Pool grows with national revenue (anti-zero-sum)
- Weights legislated, inputs publicly replicable ("any official or private citizen must be able to easily duplicate calculations")
- Simonov architecture: common federal accountability core + archipelagic modules — "differentiation is not a concession to fragmentation. It is a mechanism for substantive equality"

## Development chance ([[Chance of Economic Development With Federation Resources Planning]))

Conditional, not guaranteed. The intervening variables: equalization strength, administrative capacity, coordination quality, federal political commitment. "allocation efficiency depends primarily on institutional alignment between fiscal architecture, administrative capacity, and governance coordination" (Huseynova 2026).

## Evidence base and its limit

41-source corpus. **Honest limitation:** archipelagic evidence is Indonesia-only, and Indonesia is formally **unitary** with asymmetric decentralization — not a federation. The paper reasons from Indonesia's archipelagic fiscal mechanics to a hypothetical archipelagic federation by explicit analogy. No Philippines, Solomon Islands, Comoros, or Micronesia case material in the corpus.
