# Methodology Blueprint — deep-research Phase 1 deliverable

**Research paradigm:** Pragmatist (method selection driven by the question, not by paradigm allegiance). Secondary-data qualitative analysis with comparative case elements.

**Method:** Qualitative comparative document analysis — structured extraction from an evidence-graded local corpus, followed by thematic synthesis with an explicit failure-mode taxonomy.

**Data strategy:** Secondary only. Full corpus = 41 documents (books, journal articles, working papers, international-organization reports) held in `/Users/ivan/projects/researches/project_frp/journals/`, extracted text cached at `/tmp/fedtxt/`.

**Analytical framework:** The resource-planning chain (5 links, from Ter-Minassian 1997 Ch.1 + OECD 2013):
1. Expenditure assignment
2. Revenue assignment
3. Intergovernmental transfers (vertical gap + horizontal equalization)
4. Budgeting / PFM discipline (predictability, performance linkage)
5. Multi-year public investment planning

Each case study (Pakistan, Sudan, Ethiopia, Indonesia, plus IMF/OECD cross-country material) is scored against the chain: which links hold, which break, and why.

**Validity & reliability criteria:**
- *Construct validity:* every claim about a case anchored to a verbatim quote from a corpus source (integrity gate at Stage 2.5 enforces this).
- *Internal validity:* failure modes only asserted if documented in ≥2 independent sources OR 1 authoritative source with named mechanism (bias guard against single-source anecdotes).
- *External validity:* explicitly limited — findings transfer to developing federations with similar vertical/horizontal imbalance profiles, not claimed as universal.
- *Reliability:* verbatim quote bank with file+section pointers makes every claim re-checkable by a second reader.
- *Reflexivity:* the corpus is the user's own collection; selection bias toward fiscal-federalism literature is acknowledged and stated as a limitation.

**Bias controls (Phase 3 devil's-advocate guardrails):**
- Cherry-picking control: the synthesis quotes both successes (OECD 15-capacity framework; declining CV in Indonesia 2023-25) and failures (Sudan formula inversion; Pakistan disorderly allocation).
- Confirmation-bias control: the paper's thesis (planning beats discretion *when* rule-based and predictable) must survive the Ethiopia counter-case (decentralization before capacity = worse allocation), which is included rather than suppressed.

**Search strategy (if external supplementation becomes necessary at Stage 2):**
Databases: Google Scholar, OECD iLibrary, World Bank Open Knowledge, IMF eLibrary.
Keywords: "fiscal federalism" AND ("resource allocation" OR "intergovernmental transfer" OR "equalization"); "expenditure assignment"; "revenue assignment"; "subnational PFM predictability".
Inclusion: 1997-2026, English, peer-reviewed or IO-issued. Exclusion: non-fiscal federalism topics, single-country pure-accounting papers.
