# Future Improvement

> Known evidence gaps and next collection targets. Written from the corpus gap analysis in `.ars-pipeline/corpus_profile.md`.

## Gap 1 (critical): Non-Indonesian archipelagic federations

**The single biggest gap.** The corpus's archipelagic evidence is Indonesia-only, and Indonesia is formally **unitary** with asymmetric decentralization — not federal. The paper's central move (Indonesia → hypothetical archipelagic federation) is analogy, not comparison.

**Collect:**
- **Philippines** — formally unitary but with the Bangsamoro Autonomous Region in Muslim Mindanao (BARMM) and the Internal Revenue Allotment; the closest archipelagic asymmetric case
- **Solomon Islands, Vanuatu, Comoros, Micronesia (FSM)** — actual federations or federal-adjacent archipelagic states
- **Tanzania** — union government + Zanzibar; semi-autonomous islands in a union, informative for the asymmetric-design branch
- **Federal States of Micronesia** — true compact federacy; transfer design between national and state tiers
- Question to answer: does a federal constitution produce **different** archipelagic outcomes than Indonesia's unitary asymmetry, or do the same formula failures recur?

## Gap 2: Resource planning as a formal optimization problem

No corpus source treats resource planning as an operations-research problem (objective function, constraints, instruments across tiers). The vault's optimization framing is a synthesis built from equalization formulas, the OECD capacity framework, and Rijoly et al.'s NEG calibration.

**Collect:**
- Public-investment management literature (Rajaram et al. 2014 World Bank framework; Dabla-Norris et al. 2011 IMF efficiency index — both cited in OECD 2013)
- Planning theory applied to intergovernmental finance
- Spatial-optimization models for service delivery across fragmented territories (facility location on archipelagos)

## Gap 3: Political constraints → algorithmic rules, general treatment

Only formula-case material exists (Pakistan Box 11, DAU weights, Philippines IRA, Sudan criteria). No general treatment of how bargaining, veto players, and elite capture get **encoded in or excluded from** transfer formulas. Karapin (2020) documents veto-player dynamics (US energy) but not for fiscal formulas.

**Collect:**
- Political economy of formula design — studies of NFC negotiations (Pakistan), Finance Commission (India), CGC (Australia Commonwealth Grants Commission) as **political** processes
- Work on rule-based vs discretionary transfer systems and their durability under regime change
-LAT: literature on "fiscal rules as commitment devices" applied to intergovernmental transfers

## Gap 4: Cost-of-provision measurement

The optimization's core innovation (cost index including dispersion and connectivity) has no measured implementation in the corpus. OECD 2013 mentions capacity requirements; Al-Samarrai & Lewis 2021 document that formulas rarely adjust for cost — but no source **constructs** such an index.

**Collect:**
- Australia's CGC cost-of-service adjustments — the one real-world implementation of need equalization
- Indonesia'sD AKpNIP / fiscal capacity index methodology post-HKPD
- Small-island service-cost studies (health, education logistics)

## Gap 5: Climate-fiscal integration beyond Rijoly et al.

Rijoly, Leasiwal & Pradana (2026) is the corpus's only climate-fiscal model for archipelagos, and it is a stylized calibration, not an empirical estimate.

**Collect:**
- Empirical climate-adaptation cost estimates for archipelagic subnational governments
- Resilience-conditioned transfer designs (ex post disaster transfers vs ex ante resilience)
